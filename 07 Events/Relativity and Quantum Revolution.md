# Relativity and Quantum Revolution

> Modern physics changed because classical mechanics, electromagnetism, thermal radiation, atomic spectra, and experiments could not all fit one conceptual system; relativity and quantum theory resolved different contradictions by changing foundational assumptions.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Nineteenth-century physics had highly successful but partly incompatible theories.
- New precision experiments and spectroscopy exposed anomalies.

### Enabling conditions
- Advanced mathematics provided tools for spacetime, probability amplitudes, and operators.
- Dense international research networks accelerated challenge and replication.

## Turning points
- Planck quantized energy exchange in blackbody calculations.
- Einstein's 1905 papers addressed light quanta and special relativity.
- General relativity redefined gravity geometrically.
- Bohr, Heisenberg, Schrodinger, Born, Dirac, and others built quantum mechanics in the 1920s.

## Why it changed the system
- Electronics, nuclear physics, lasers, semiconductors, GPS corrections, and modern chemistry depend on the resulting frameworks.
- Intuition about determinism, measurement, space, and time changed profoundly.

## Competing interpretation

Calling it an Einstein-only revolution erases a network of competing formulations and experiments; different problems required different conceptual changes.

## Counterfactual

Even without Einstein, Lorentz/Poincare and others likely move relativity forward, but the synthesis and timing may differ.
