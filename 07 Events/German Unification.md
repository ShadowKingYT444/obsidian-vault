# German Unification

> German unification under Prussia came from a conjunction of nationalism, economic integration, Prussian military-fiscal reform, Austrian weakness, and Bismarck's sequencing of limited conflicts.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- The German Confederation contained many states with growing economic ties through the Zollverein.
- Prussia and Austria competed for leadership while nationalism increased demand for some form of unity.

### Enabling conditions
- Prussian army reforms and rail mobilization improved military capability.
- Bismarck could exploit rival powers' conflicting interests to isolate opponents temporarily.

## Turning points
- War with Denmark in 1864 created a joint problem with Austria.
- The 1866 Austro-Prussian War excluded Austria from German political leadership; peace terms were deliberately limited.
- The Franco-Prussian War rallied southern German states into a new empire proclaimed in 1871.

## Why it changed the system
- A powerful German Empire altered Europe's balance.
- The new state combined national institutions with Prussian military-monarchical structures and federal components.

## Competing interpretation

Bismarck mattered, but unification was not created from nothing by 'blood and iron'; economic, nationalist, military, and institutional trends preceded him.

## Counterfactual

A punitive peace against Austria in 1866 could produce a durable anti-Prussian coalition and make 1870 unification harder.
