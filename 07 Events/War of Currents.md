# War of Currents

> AC beat early DC networks for large-scale distribution because system economics favored voltage transformation and long-distance transmission; patents, finance, safety narratives, and installed infrastructure shaped the transition.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Electric lighting demand was rising while generation/distribution networks were local and capital intensive.
- Transmission losses made low-voltage long-distance DC expensive.

### Enabling conditions
- Transformers allowed AC voltage to step up for transmission and down for local use.
- Polyphase motors made AC useful for power as well as lighting.

## Turning points
- Westinghouse licensed Tesla's polyphase patents and built AC capability.
- Public disputes over safety became commercial and political weapons.
- The 1893 World's Columbian Exposition and Niagara projects demonstrated large AC systems.

## Why it changed the system
- AC became dominant for grid distribution, while later power systems used AC and DC in different layers.
- Electrical standards and infrastructure created deep path dependence.

## Competing interpretation

The 'Edison vs Tesla' duel is too personal; Westinghouse, engineers, transformers, utilities, capital, regulation, and load economics are central.

## Counterfactual

Without efficient transformers and AC motors, DC's local-network model remains more competitive despite transmission limits.
