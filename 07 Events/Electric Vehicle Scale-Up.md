# Electric Vehicle Scale-Up

> EV adoption accelerated when battery cost, energy density, charging, software, manufacturing, policy, and consumer demand crossed thresholds together; no car company acts independently of the battery and grid ecosystem.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Lithium-ion production expanded through consumer electronics before mass-market EVs.
- Climate and air-quality policy increased pressure to decarbonize transport.

### Enabling conditions
- Battery manufacturing learning lowered pack costs.
- Power electronics and software improved drivetrain control while charging networks reduced range friction.

## Turning points
- Premium EVs demonstrated consumer demand beyond small compliance vehicles.
- Higher-volume models forced new battery and manufacturing investments.
- China became the largest EV manufacturing and market ecosystem, intensifying cost competition.
- Major automakers committed large capital to EV platforms with mixed execution.

## Why it changed the system
- Automotive supply chains shifted toward cells, minerals, power electronics, and software.
- Grid demand, charging standards, mineral sourcing, and battery recycling became strategic infrastructure problems.

## Competing interpretation

A Tesla-only story ignores decades of battery science, Asian cell manufacturing, regulation, Chinese industrial policy, and charging/grid complements.

## Counterfactual

Cheap batteries without charging access or manufacturing scale would improve vehicles but slow mass adoption.
