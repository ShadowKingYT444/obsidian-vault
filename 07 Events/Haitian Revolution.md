# Haitian Revolution

> The only successful large-scale slave revolution destroyed one of the world's richest plantation slave systems and created an independent state while fighting civil war, European intervention, and racial hierarchy.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Saint-Domingue's wealth depended on brutal plantation slavery and a demographic majority of enslaved Africans.
- Free people of color, white planters, metropolitan officials, and enslaved populations had conflicting claims after the French Revolution.

### Enabling conditions
- Revolutionary rights language destabilized old legal categories.
- European great-power war divided and distracted colonial powers.

## Turning points
- The 1791 slave uprising made restoration of plantation order vastly more difficult.
- French abolition in 1794 and Toussaint Louverture's alliance shifts transformed the war.
- Napoleon's expedition attempted to restore metropolitan control; resistance and disease destroyed much of the force.
- Independence was declared in 1804.

## Why it changed the system
- Haiti ended slavery and French rule, transforming Atlantic politics.
- Foreign isolation, indemnity to France, war destruction, and plantation legacies constrained the new state's economy.

## Competing interpretation

The revolution cannot be reduced to French ideas; enslaved people's organization, African political cultures, military experience, and local conditions were causal.

## Counterfactual

A French decision not to restore coercive plantation control could alter the independence path, but mass emancipation had already changed the political equilibrium.
