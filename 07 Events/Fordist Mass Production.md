# Fordist Mass Production

> Ford's cost breakthrough came from product standardization plus flow production, specialized tooling, supply coordination, and labor stabilization; the same rigidity later reduced responsiveness to changing demand.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Growing US incomes and road networks created potential mass automobile demand.
- Machine tools and interchangeable-part practices had matured across industries.

### Enabling conditions
- A standardized Model T reduced changeover and part complexity.
- Large plants justified specialized equipment and process engineering.

## Turning points
- Moving assembly methods spread through Highland Park from 1913.
- Production time and unit cost fell sharply as flow improved.
- The five-dollar day helped reduce turnover in an exhausting labor system.
- Model T dominance eroded as consumers demanded more variety and competitors improved.

## Why it changed the system
- Mass production became a model across manufacturing.
- Factory work became more productive but also more repetitive, controlled, and conflict-prone.

## Competing interpretation

The moving line was not a single Ford invention; it assembled prior ideas from meatpacking, machine production, and internal experimentation.

## Counterfactual

Without product standardization, a moving line faces too much variability to deliver the same throughput gains.
