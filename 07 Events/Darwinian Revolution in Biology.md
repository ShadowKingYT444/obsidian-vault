# Darwinian Revolution in Biology

> Evolution by natural selection became powerful because it offered a generative mechanism connecting biogeography, adaptation, fossils, variation, and common descent; genetics later repaired a major missing piece.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Naturalists had accumulated global specimens and evidence that species and Earth changed over time.
- Geology expanded plausible time scales.

### Enabling conditions
- Breeding practices supplied visible examples of cumulative selection.
- Scientific publishing and specimen networks allowed comparison across regions.

## Turning points
- Darwin's Beagle observations and later synthesis built a large evidence base.
- Wallace independently formulated natural selection, prompting the 1858 joint presentation.
- Origin of Species in 1859 made the mechanism central to debate.
- Mendelian genetics and population genetics later produced the modern synthesis.

## Why it changed the system
- Biology gained a unifying historical mechanism.
- Evolutionary ideas were also misapplied in racist and social ideologies that did not follow from biological theory.

## Competing interpretation

Darwin was central but not alone; Wallace, breeders, geologists, taxonomists, and later geneticists changed the theory substantially.

## Counterfactual

Without genetics, natural selection remains compelling but inheritance is theoretically unstable; the modern synthesis solves a problem Darwin could not.
