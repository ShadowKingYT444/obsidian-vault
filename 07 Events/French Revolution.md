# French Revolution

> Fiscal crisis triggered a constitutional breakdown that became a social and political revolution; war and internal conflict then radicalized institutions and created forms of mass mobilization Napoleon later inherited.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- The monarchy had a chronic fiscal problem and politically protected privilege.
- Enlightenment arguments, social hierarchy, food-price pressure, and institutional conflict weakened old legitimacy.

### Enabling conditions
- The Estates-General created a venue where representation disputes could become sovereignty claims.
- Print and urban political clubs accelerated mass participation.

## Turning points
- The National Assembly claimed constituent authority and feudal privileges were attacked.
- The monarchy fell amid war and political radicalization.
- The Terror linked emergency governance, civil conflict, and mass mobilization.
- The Directory stabilized some gains but remained politically weak.

## Why it changed the system
- Legal privilege and seigneurial structures were transformed; citizenship and nationalism expanded as political categories.
- Military mobilization and administrative reform increased the scale of French war-making.

## Competing interpretation

No single class or ideology explains the Revolution; fiscal institutions, popular politics, elite division, war, and contingency interacted.

## Counterfactual

A credible fiscal settlement before 1789 could preserve monarchy longer, but it would still require powerful groups to accept redistribution of taxation and authority.
