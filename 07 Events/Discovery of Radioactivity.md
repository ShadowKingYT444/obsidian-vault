# Discovery of Radioactivity

> Radioactivity became a new field because precise measurement revealed that some materials emitted energy in ways chemical composition and classical atom concepts could not explain.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- X-rays and cathode-ray research had made invisible radiation a live experimental problem.
- Electrometers and photographic methods could detect weak effects.

### Enabling conditions
- Becquerel identified uranium radiation.
- Pierre and Marie Curie's instrumentation allowed quantitative comparison of activity.

## Turning points
- Curie found pitchblende more active than uranium alone, suggesting unknown elements.
- Polonium and radium were identified through difficult chemical separation.
- Rutherford and others distinguished radiation types and developed nuclear transformation models.

## Why it changed the system
- The atom ceased to look indivisible and chemically static.
- Nuclear physics, radiometric dating, medicine, and later nuclear technology grew from the field.

## Competing interpretation

The dramatic radium story should not hide the network of chemists, instrument makers, mines, and later theoretical work needed to interpret the signal.

## Counterfactual

Without precise electrical measurement, unusual ores look chemically messy rather than evidence for a new atomic phenomenon.
