# Personal Computing Revolution

> Personal computing emerged when microprocessors, memory, displays, software abstractions, manufacturing, and user-interface ideas crossed cost thresholds; platform ecosystems mattered as much as any one machine.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Semiconductor scaling steadily reduced computation cost and size.
- Large computing institutions had already developed operating systems, networking, and interactive interfaces.

### Enabling conditions
- Microprocessors made general-purpose computers affordable to small firms and individuals.
- Global electronics manufacturing and standardized components lowered hardware barriers.

## Turning points
- Early hobbyist microcomputers proved demand outside institutions.
- Apple II and IBM PC expanded business/consumer markets through different architectures.
- Graphical interfaces made interaction more visual, while Microsoft and Intel ecosystems scaled broadly.
- Laptops, broadband, and smartphones blurred the boundary between personal computer and network terminal.

## Why it changed the system
- Software became a mass industry and computing moved into homes, schools, offices, and pockets.
- Platform lock-in and winner-take-most dynamics became central business forces.

## Competing interpretation

Xerox PARC, Apple, Microsoft, IBM, semiconductor firms, open-source communities, and Asian manufacturers all matter; 'one visionary invented the PC' is false.

## Counterfactual

Without semiconductor cost decline, interface innovation remains trapped in expensive institutional machines.
