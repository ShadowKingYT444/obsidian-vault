# Congress of Vienna

> The Vienna settlement worked not by restoring a frozen old order but by balancing major powers, reintegrating France, and creating consultation practices that made limited revision less dangerous than exclusion.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Europe had experienced two decades of revolutionary and Napoleonic war.
- No single victor could cheaply dominate the continent after France's defeat.

### Enabling conditions
- Major powers had learned that durable coalitions required negotiated interests, not only battlefield victory.
- Diplomats could use territorial compensation and dynastic legitimacy as bargaining tools.

## Turning points
- Disputes over Poland and Saxony nearly split the victors.
- Talleyrand used France's diplomatic reentry to exploit divisions and restore status.
- Napoleon's Hundred Days briefly reunited the coalition and reinforced settlement urgency.

## Why it changed the system
- France was not permanently destroyed or partitioned, reducing incentives for immediate revanchism.
- The Concert of Europe created repeated great-power consultation, though it did not prevent all wars or suppress nationalism.

## Competing interpretation

The settlement is often praised for a century of peace, but Europe still saw major wars; its stronger achievement was managing great-power competition more flexibly than punitive exclusion might have.

## Counterfactual

A Carthaginian peace that permanently humiliates France could create stronger revisionist incentives and make coalition enforcement more expensive.
