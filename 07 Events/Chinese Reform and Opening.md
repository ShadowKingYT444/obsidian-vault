# Chinese Reform and Opening

> China changed incentives and openness through staged, often local experimentation while retaining one-party political control; growth came from reform interacting with prior capacity, global markets, demographics, and investment.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Mao-era institutions had built basic state reach and human-capital gains but produced severe economic inefficiencies and past political disasters.
- Rural collective incentives and industrial allocation systems constrained productivity.

### Enabling conditions
- Local governments had enough discretion to test arrangements.
- Hong Kong, Taiwan, overseas Chinese networks, and a growing global trading system supplied capital, markets, and know-how.

## Turning points
- Household responsibility practices spread in agriculture.
- Special economic zones concentrated foreign-oriented experimentation.
- Township and village enterprises expanded outside classic state planning.
- Deng's 1992 southern tour reinforced market-oriented reforms after post-1989 hesitation.

## Why it changed the system
- China experienced decades of rapid growth, urbanization, and global integration.
- Inequality, corruption, migration stress, environmental damage, and political repression accompanied the transformation.

## Competing interpretation

Calling reform pure 'capitalism' or pure state planning misses the hybrid, iterative institutions and changing local incentives.

## Counterfactual

A big-bang national liberalization without administrative continuity could create faster dislocation and political backlash; gradualism traded clarity for experimentation.
