# Seven Years War

> Often called the first truly global war, the conflict linked European balance-of-power fighting with colonial, naval, and commercial competition; fiscal systems and coalition endurance mattered as much as battlefield command.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Britain and France competed for colonial and maritime power while Austria sought to recover Silesia from Prussia.
- European alliances realigned in the Diplomatic Revolution, creating new threat combinations.

### Enabling conditions
- Britain could use public credit and naval power to subsidize allies and attack overseas commerce.
- Prussia's centralized military-fiscal system supported rapid mobilization but had little strategic depth.

## Turning points
- Frederick's invasion of Saxony opened a high-risk continental war.
- British victories in 1759 shifted several colonial and naval theaters.
- Russia's 1762 change of ruler removed a major threat to Prussia at a critical moment.

## Why it changed the system
- Britain gained major imperial territory but also debt that shaped later colonial taxation disputes.
- Prussia survived and gained great-power status.
- France lost much of its North American position but remained a major European power.

## Competing interpretation

Frederick's skill mattered, but Prussian survival also depended on coalition fractures and contingency; global outcomes depended strongly on British finance and sea power.

## Counterfactual

If Russia remains fully committed in 1762, Prussia's survival becomes much less likely despite Frederick's operational ability.
