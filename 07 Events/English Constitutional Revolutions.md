# English Constitutional Revolutions

> Conflict over taxation, religion, sovereignty, and armed authority produced civil war, regicide, republic, restoration, and finally a monarchy more constrained by Parliament and a stronger fiscal-financial state.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- The crown had growing fiscal needs but weak independent taxation capacity.
- Religious reforms created conflict across England, Scotland, and Ireland.

### Enabling conditions
- Parliament controlled important taxation channels and could mobilize local political networks.
- Print expanded political argument and mobilization.

## Turning points
- War began in 1642 after repeated constitutional breakdown.
- The New Model Army became an unusually effective military-political institution.
- Charles I was executed in 1649; monarchy returned in 1660.
- The 1688–89 settlement removed James II and strengthened parliamentary conditions on monarchy.

## Why it changed the system
- Parliamentary taxation and public credit later supported a stronger fiscal-military state.
- Political rights remained highly unequal despite constitutional change.

## Competing interpretation

A linear 'rise of democracy' story is false; the period involved religious coercion, conquest in Ireland, military rule, and elite bargaining.

## Counterfactual

A crown able to tax independently would face fewer incentives to bargain with Parliament, potentially producing a different constitutional equilibrium.
