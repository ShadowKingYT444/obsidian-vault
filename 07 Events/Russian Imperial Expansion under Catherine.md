# Russian Imperial Expansion under Catherine

> Catherine expanded Russia's territory and prestige while governing through elite bargains that limited how far Enlightenment reform could challenge serfdom and noble privilege.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Russia had a large military state but uneven administration across vast territory.
- Ottoman weakness and Polish political fragmentation created expansion opportunities.

### Enabling conditions
- Noble service networks and army modernization gave the crown coercive reach.
- European diplomatic competition allowed Russia to bargain and intervene.

## Turning points
- Wars with the Ottoman Empire opened Black Sea access.
- Pugachev's rebellion reinforced fear of rural social revolution.
- Partitions of Poland extended Russian rule westward.
- The Charter to the Nobility entrenched elite privileges.

## Why it changed the system
- Russia became more deeply involved in European great-power politics.
- Territorial growth increased diversity and administrative burden while serfdom remained central.

## Competing interpretation

Enlightened rhetoric and autocratic social order coexisted; reform should be evaluated by institutional changes, not correspondence with philosophes.

## Counterfactual

A ruler who attacks noble privilege immediately after a coup risks losing the coalition that secures the throne.
