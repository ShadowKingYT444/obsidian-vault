# Tokugawa Settlement of Japan

> Tokugawa stability came from converting victory into a system that constrained daimyo, standardized status relations, controlled strategic movement, and made rebellion costly without eliminating local administration.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- The Sengoku period had produced militarized domains and repeated coalition turnover.
- No ruler could cheaply administer all local affairs directly from the center.

### Enabling conditions
- Oda and Toyotomi unification had already reduced independent warfare.
- Land surveys, castle control, and warrior bureaucracies created tools for supervision.

## Turning points
- Sekigahara in 1600 established Tokugawa supremacy.
- The shogunate formalized relations among domains, court, temples, and warrior houses.
- Later alternate attendance required daimyo presence and expenditure connected to Edo.

## Why it changed the system
- Large-scale internal war largely disappeared for more than two centuries.
- Domain autonomy remained, producing a layered rather than fully centralized state.

## Competing interpretation

Calling Tokugawa Japan simply 'closed' is misleading; external trade was controlled and channeled, not absent.

## Counterfactual

Without institutions that constrain daimyo after Ieyasu, succession could reopen the coalition game that produced Sengoku war.
