# Renaissance

> The Renaissance is best understood as overlapping changes in patronage, classical-text recovery, urban wealth, artistic technique, education, and print—not a clean break from a uniformly 'dark' medieval period.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Italian city-states concentrated commercial wealth and competitive patronage.
- Byzantine and Islamic transmission preserved and expanded many classical texts before western European recovery.

### Enabling conditions
- Humanist education created demand for classical languages and manuscripts.
- Workshop systems connected art, engineering, architecture, and craft knowledge.

## Turning points
- Petrarch and later humanists made classical textual recovery a prestigious program.
- Perspective, anatomy, and oil-paint techniques changed artistic representation.
- Print greatly increased later humanist diffusion.

## Why it changed the system
- Classical rhetoric and political history reshaped education and elite culture.
- Artistic and technical practices increasingly emphasized direct observation and mathematical representation.

## Competing interpretation

The 'rebirth after darkness' narrative is misleading because medieval intellectual and technical continuity was substantial.

## Counterfactual

Without print, Renaissance humanism still exists but diffuses more slowly and remains more dependent on elite manuscript networks.
