# Italian Wars and Renaissance Statecraft

> Foreign invasions exposed the weakness of fragmented Italian states and created the political environment in which Machiavelli analyzed military dependence, institutional strength, and adaptation.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Rich Italian city-states competed intensely but lacked a stable collective security system.
- France, Spain, and the Habsburgs could deploy larger dynastic military resources into Italy.

### Enabling conditions
- Mercenary forces and shifting alliances made commitments flexible but unreliable.
- Gunpowder siege warfare changed the economics of fortified politics.

## Turning points
- Charles VIII's 1494 invasion disrupted the existing Italian balance.
- Repeated French-Habsburg wars turned Italy into a major contest theater.
- The Sack of Rome in 1527 symbolized the vulnerability of Italian political arrangements.

## Why it changed the system
- Spanish/Habsburg influence increased across Italy.
- Warfare accelerated fortification and military-administrative change.

## Competing interpretation

Machiavelli's ideas make more sense as responses to a specific institutional failure environment than as timeless advice to be ruthless.

## Counterfactual

A durable Italian defensive federation could raise invasion costs, but internal rivalries made credible commitment difficult.
