# Black Death

> The Black Death was not only a mortality shock; by changing labor scarcity, bargaining power, state revenue, religious confidence, and settlement patterns, it altered institutions differently across regions.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Dense trade networks moved plague across Eurasia and the Mediterranean.
- Agrarian societies had high dependence on labor and limited medical understanding.

### Enabling conditions
- Ports, caravan routes, armies, and grain trade connected disease ecologies.
- Preexisting malnutrition and local political conditions affected vulnerability.

## Turning points
- Mortality reached extraordinary levels in many localities between 1347 and 1351.
- Labor scarcity increased wage pressure and challenged customary obligations in parts of western Europe.
- Authorities tried wage controls, quarantine, religious measures, and persecution with mixed results.

## Why it changed the system
- In some regions peasants and workers gained bargaining power; in others elites tightened coercion.
- Public-health institutions and quarantine practices developed unevenly over later outbreaks.

## Competing interpretation

Simple claims that plague 'caused capitalism' are too strong; demographic shock interacted with land/labor ratios, institutions, urbanization, and political bargaining.

## Counterfactual

Where elites had greater coercive capacity, the same mortality shock could strengthen labor controls rather than weaken them.
