# Alexander's Conquest of Persia

> Macedonian military reform and political cohesion met a wealthy but difficult-to-coordinate empire; Alexander converted battlefield victory into rapid regime transfer, then outran the institutional problem of succession.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Philip II had created a professional combined-arms army and subordinated much of Greece.
- The Achaemenid Empire was vast and resource-rich but had to coordinate satrapies and armies across long distances.

### Enabling conditions
- Macedon had siege capability, elite cavalry, disciplined infantry, and experienced commanders.
- Persian political structure allowed local elites to be incorporated after central defeats.

## Turning points
- Granicus opened Asia Minor.
- Issus displaced Darius from the western theater; Tyre and Egypt secured the coast.
- Gaugamela in 331 BCE shattered Darius' ability to defend the imperial center.

## Why it changed the system
- Achaemenid rule ended and Hellenistic successor kingdoms spread Greek-Macedonian political and cultural institutions.
- The empire fragmented after Alexander's death because succession and command authority were unresolved.

## Competing interpretation

Older 'great conqueror' narratives center Alexander; stronger analysis treats Philip's reforms, Persian structure, elite bargaining, and successor institutions as causal too.

## Counterfactual

A clear adult heir and succession mechanism might have delayed fragmentation, but the empire's distance and commander power would still make unity expensive.
