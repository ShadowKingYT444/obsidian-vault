# US Constitutional Founding

> The Constitution replaced a weak confederation with stronger fiscal and executive capacity while dividing authority to reduce fears of centralized power; the settlement also protected slavery through major compromises.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- The Articles of Confederation gave Congress weak taxation and enforcement tools.
- War debt, interstate trade conflict, frontier security, and Shays' Rebellion increased elite concern about state weakness.

### Enabling conditions
- State constitutional experience supplied competing institutional models.
- Political leaders could bargain in a convention protected from immediate public negotiation.

## Turning points
- The Philadelphia Convention shifted from amendment to replacement.
- Representation and slavery compromises made ratification coalition possible.
- The Federalist/Anti-Federalist debate produced commitments that contributed to the Bill of Rights.

## Why it changed the system
- Federal taxation, courts, executive power, and national credit strengthened the state.
- Federalism and separation of powers created durable veto points and path dependence.

## Competing interpretation

The founding combined genuine institutional innovation with elite interest protection and slavery; neither pure democratic triumph nor pure class conspiracy is sufficient.

## Counterfactual

A confederation unable to tax reliably faces chronic credit and security constraints, but a much stronger central constitution might fail ratification.
