# Progressive Era State Building

> Industrial concentration and urban growth produced demands for new regulatory and administrative capacity; reforms expanded expertise and federal power while leaving racial exclusion and coercive labor structures largely intact.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- National corporations and cities had outgrown many local regulatory tools.
- Political machines, corruption concerns, labor conflict, and public-health problems created reform coalitions.

### Enabling conditions
- Professional associations, investigative journalism, universities, and civil-service reforms supplied expertise.
- Mass media made national agenda setting easier.

## Turning points
- Antitrust enforcement became more active.
- Food/drug and railroad regulation expanded federal oversight.
- Conservation and administrative commissions increased technocratic governance.
- Constitutional amendments changed taxation and senator selection.

## Why it changed the system
- The US state gained lasting regulatory and fiscal capacity.
- Reform often excluded Black Americans and could support eugenic or paternalistic policies.

## Competing interpretation

Progressivism was not one coherent ideology; business regulation, democracy reform, social control, and racism could coexist.

## Counterfactual

Without federal administrative growth, national firms would face a patchwork of state controls and potentially greater private coordination power.
