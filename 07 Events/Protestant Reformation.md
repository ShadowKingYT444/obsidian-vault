# Protestant Reformation

> The Reformation became durable because theological disputes aligned with printing, state interests, urban networks, institutional grievances, and coercive power; doctrine alone does not explain its geography.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Late medieval church institutions faced criticism over authority, finance, and practice.
- Princes and cities had incentives to contest external ecclesiastical jurisdiction.

### Enabling conditions
- Printing allowed rapid distribution of polemic and vernacular texts.
- Universities and urban networks provided trained readers and organizers.

## Turning points
- Luther's dispute over indulgences escalated into a challenge to papal authority.
- Political protection by German princes prevented immediate suppression.
- Confessional states and wars made religious identity part of state formation.

## Why it changed the system
- Western Christianity fragmented institutionally.
- Education, censorship, state authority, and confessional discipline intensified in Protestant and Catholic regions.

## Competing interpretation

Neither 'pure theology' nor 'princes wanted church land' is enough; belief and institutional incentives interacted.

## Counterfactual

Without print and princely protection, Luther might have been contained more like earlier reform movements.
