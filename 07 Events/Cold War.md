# Cold War

> US-Soviet rivalry was constrained by nuclear deterrence while displaced into alliances, proxy wars, technology competition, economic systems, covert action, and ideological legitimacy across a decolonizing world.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- World War II left the US and USSR with unmatched military reach and incompatible security/political systems.
- Nuclear weapons raised the expected cost of direct great-power war to unprecedented levels.

### Enabling conditions
- Alliance systems, intelligence agencies, aid programs, and global basing extended competition.
- Decolonization created new states whose interests did not reduce to superpower ideology.

## Turning points
- The Berlin crises and NATO/Warsaw Pact institutionalized European division.
- Korean and Vietnam wars showed how rivalry escalated at the periphery.
- The Cuban Missile Crisis exposed nuclear escalation risk.
- Sino-Soviet split, détente, renewed competition, and Soviet economic/political crisis changed the balance.
- Eastern European revolutions and Soviet dissolution ended the bipolar order.

## Why it changed the system
- Nuclear deterrence, proxy wars, coups, arms races, and development aid shaped global institutions.
- Postcolonial politics were often distorted when local conflicts were interpreted mainly through superpower competition.

## Competing interpretation

The Cold War was simultaneously ideological, geopolitical, nuclear, economic, and imperial; assigning single-side causation varies sharply by period and theater.

## Counterfactual

Without nuclear weapons, direct military clashes among great powers likely become easier to contemplate; with them, competition shifts toward bounded or indirect forms.
