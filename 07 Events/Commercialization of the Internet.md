# Commercialization of the Internet

> The internet became a general economic platform when open protocols met personal computers, fiber/telecom networks, browsers, search, data centers, payments, venture capital, and business models.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Research networks had already developed packet switching and TCP/IP.
- Personal computers created millions of potential endpoints outside research institutions.

### Enabling conditions
- Open standards reduced permission needed to build new applications.
- Falling compute/storage costs and telecom deregulation expanded capacity.

## Turning points
- The World Wide Web simplified publishing and navigation.
- Commercial restrictions on backbone use were removed and ISPs expanded.
- Browsers and search engines reduced discovery friction.
- Cloud infrastructure later lowered the fixed cost of deploying services.

## Why it changed the system
- Communication, media, retail, advertising, software distribution, and labor markets were reorganized.
- Network effects created very large platform firms and new concentration/regulation problems.

## Competing interpretation

Neither government research nor private entrepreneurship alone explains the internet economy; public infrastructure, open standards, private capital, and complementary innovation interacted.

## Counterfactual

A closed proprietary network stack could fragment the ecosystem and sharply raise application-layer innovation costs.
