# End of Apartheid and South African Transition

> Apartheid ended through mass resistance, economic pressure, elite bargaining, international isolation, security stalemate, and changing Cold War conditions; Mandela and de Klerk mattered at a moment when structural incentives had shifted.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Apartheid required escalating coercion against a politically mobilized Black majority.
- Sanctions, capital pressure, labor unrest, township resistance, and regional war raised the cost of the status quo.

### Enabling conditions
- The ANC and other movements had organizational legitimacy despite repression and exile.
- Business and state elites increasingly doubted that apartheid could remain economically and politically stable.

## Turning points
- De Klerk unbanned liberation movements and released Mandela in 1990.
- Negotiations proceeded amid severe political violence and repeated breakdown risk.
- The 1994 election created majority rule under an interim constitutional framework.

## Why it changed the system
- Formal apartheid ended and democratic institutions were established.
- Economic inequality, spatial segregation, and state-capacity problems persisted.

## Competing interpretation

The 'two great men reconciled a nation' story is incomplete; millions of organizers, workers, activists, security actors, international networks, and institutions changed the bargaining space.

## Counterfactual

If either side expects outright military victory, negotiated transition becomes less attractive and political violence likely rises.
