# Fall of the Roman Republic

> The Republic's competitive elite institutions were destabilized by empire-scale wealth, military patronage, inequality, and repeated norm-breaking; Caesar accelerated the crisis but did not create it alone.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Provincial conquest increased wealth, corruption opportunities, and military commands.
- Citizen-soldier assumptions strained as long campaigns and land concentration changed social conditions.
- Republican offices were designed for a city-state elite, not sustained administration of a Mediterranean empire.

### Enabling conditions
- Political violence from the Gracchi onward normalized exceptional measures.
- Marius, Sulla, Pompey, Crassus, and others linked armies more closely to personal political careers.

## Turning points
- Sulla's march on Rome showed that armies could settle constitutional disputes.
- The First Triumvirate bypassed normal elite competition through private coalition.
- Caesar's Rubicon crossing led to civil war; his assassination failed to restore the old equilibrium.
- Octavian's victory over Antony removed peer military competitors.

## Why it changed the system
- Augustus created a durable one-man system wrapped in republican forms.
- The Senate and magistracies remained but lost much independent strategic power.

## Competing interpretation

The key dispute is how much weight to give socioeconomic structure, elite choices, military professionalization, and contingency.

## Counterfactual

Removing Caesar likely changes sequence and timing, but the prior pattern of military-backed elite competition makes simple restoration unlikely.
