# Mughal Consolidation under Akbar

> Akbar expanded Mughal power by combining conquest with elite incorporation, ranked service, revenue reform, and a political identity broad enough to govern religious and regional diversity.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- North India contained powerful regional states and landed elites.
- Mughal rule needed reliable revenue and military service across large distances.

### Enabling conditions
- Gunpowder warfare and cavalry traditions supported imperial campaigns.
- Rajput alliances and experienced Persianate administrators widened the talent base.

## Turning points
- Early regency victories stabilized Akbar's throne.
- Rajput partnerships reduced resistance in key regions.
- Mansabdari and revenue reforms tied rank, service, and fiscal assessment more closely to the center.

## Why it changed the system
- The Mughal Empire became one of the world's largest and wealthiest early modern states.
- Later rulers inherited both a scalable imperial framework and unresolved center-periphery tensions.

## Competing interpretation

Religious tolerance was significant, but it also functioned as statecraft in a diverse empire and should not be reduced to modern liberal categories.

## Counterfactual

A policy of systematic exclusion toward major Hindu elites would make imperial expansion more militarily expensive and administratively thin.
