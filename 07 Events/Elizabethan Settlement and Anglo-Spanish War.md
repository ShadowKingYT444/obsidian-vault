# Elizabethan Settlement and Anglo-Spanish War

> Elizabethan England survived religious division and stronger continental rivals through a negotiated church settlement, faction management, maritime capacity, and gradual escalation rather than one decisive 'Armada miracle.'

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- England faced Catholic-Protestant division and a contested royal succession.
- Spain had greater imperial resources but commitments across Europe and the Atlantic.

### Enabling conditions
- Royal council government and Parliament gave the crown established administrative channels.
- Privateering, shipbuilding, and Atlantic commerce expanded maritime skill and incentives.

## Turning points
- The 1559 settlement established a Protestant church with deliberate ambiguities.
- English support for Dutch rebels and privateering escalated conflict.
- The 1588 Armada failed because of English resistance, Spanish operational constraints, weather, and a difficult return route.

## Why it changed the system
- Protestant succession became more secure, though religious coercion continued.
- Naval mythology later overstated a clean shift to immediate English sea supremacy.

## Competing interpretation

Elizabeth's moderation was strategic but not modern toleration; Catholics and separatist Protestants still faced repression.

## Counterfactual

A successful Spanish landing would require more than winning at sea: control of the Channel, coordination with Parma's army, and sustainable logistics.
