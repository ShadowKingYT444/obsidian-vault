# Thirty Years War

> A Bohemian constitutional-religious crisis expanded into a European power struggle because dynastic, confessional, and security interests overlapped; armies financed themselves partly through extraction, magnifying civilian devastation.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- The Holy Roman Empire contained overlapping jurisdictions and confessional settlements.
- Habsburg power raised security concerns for France, Sweden, Denmark, and German princes.

### Enabling conditions
- Military entrepreneurs could raise armies through credit and contributions.
- Confessional identities mobilized populations but did not map cleanly onto alliances.

## Turning points
- The Bohemian revolt triggered Habsburg intervention.
- Danish and Swedish entries changed the scale of war.
- Catholic France's direct intervention against the Habsburgs exposed the limits of a religion-only explanation.

## Why it changed the system
- The Peace of Westphalia adjusted imperial constitutional relations and interstate settlements.
- War devastation varied dramatically by region and became a benchmark for early modern military-fiscal strain.

## Competing interpretation

Westphalia did not invent modern sovereignty in one moment; that textbook story overstates the treaty's novelty.

## Counterfactual

A contained settlement of the Bohemian crisis could avoid continental escalation, but Habsburg-Bourbon and Baltic power competition would remain.
