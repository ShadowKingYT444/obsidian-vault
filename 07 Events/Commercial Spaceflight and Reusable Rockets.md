# Commercial Spaceflight and Reusable Rockets

> Reusable orbital launch became economically relevant only when vehicle design, engines, guidance, software, high launch cadence, landing operations, and customer demand were developed as one system.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Government space programs proved orbital launch but traditional procurement often produced high fixed and per-mission costs.
- Commercial satellite and government launch demand created a market for lower-cost providers.

### Enabling conditions
- Modern avionics, simulation, materials, computing, and private capital reduced barriers for new firms.
- NASA cargo/crew programs supplied milestone-based demand and technical interfaces.

## Turning points
- Falcon 1 reached orbit after three failures.
- Falcon 9 established higher launch cadence and NASA/commercial missions.
- First-stage landing and reflight turned reusability from demonstration into operating practice.
- Starlink created internal launch demand at exceptional scale.

## Why it changed the system
- Launch prices and cadence pressure changed global space-industry competition.
- Vertical integration and reusable hardware became credible design options, though full cost comparisons remain complex.

## Competing interpretation

The story is not one founder willing rockets into existence; NASA policy, thousands of engineers, suppliers, capital, computing, and accumulated aerospace knowledge are causal.

## Counterfactual

Reusability without enough flight cadence may fail economically because recovery hardware and refurbishment add cost that is not amortized.
