# American Civil War

> Secession to protect slavery triggered a war in which Union material capacity became decisive only after political and military leadership converted it into sustained mobilization; emancipation transformed both war aims and manpower.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Slavery's expansion and political power produced escalating sectional conflict.
- The North had larger population, industry, rail, finance, and naval capacity; the Confederacy fought defensively over vast territory.

### Enabling conditions
- Both sides mobilized mass armies and rail/telegraph systems.
- Enslaved people undermined slavery through escape, labor withdrawal, intelligence, and military service.

## Turning points
- Fort Sumter triggered open war.
- The Emancipation Proclamation linked Union victory to slavery's destruction and enabled expanded Black enlistment.
- Vicksburg and Gettysburg in 1863 changed strategic momentum.
- Grant's coordinated 1864–65 campaigns sustained pressure across theaters until Confederate collapse.

## Why it changed the system
- Slavery was constitutionally abolished; federal authority expanded.
- Reconstruction opened citizenship and voting rights but faced violent white-supremacist resistance and later rollback.

## Competing interpretation

A 'states' rights' explanation that omits slavery contradicts secession documents and political context; military victory alone did not determine the postwar racial order.

## Counterfactual

If the Confederacy can prolong war until Northern political will breaks in 1864, independence becomes more plausible despite weaker material capacity.
