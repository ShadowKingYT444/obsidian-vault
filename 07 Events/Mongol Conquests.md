# Mongol Conquests

> Mongol expansion combined steppe mobility with disciplined organization, intelligence, adaptation, and incorporation of conquered expertise; its scale also produced succession and coordination problems that pushed the empire toward khanate division.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Eurasian steppe politics rewarded mounted mobility and coalition formation.
- Many target states were politically divided or poorly coordinated with neighbors.

### Enabling conditions
- Multiple remounts per warrior, composite bows, signaling, scouting, and decimal organization supported fast operations.
- Conquered engineers and administrators expanded siege and governance capability.

## Turning points
- Unification under Temujin created a larger military-political coalition in 1206.
- Campaigns against Jin, Khwarazm, Rus' principalities, and others demonstrated adaptation across environments.
- Succession disputes and geographic scale gradually made a single center less workable.

## Why it changed the system
- Conquest caused enormous destruction but also intensified long-distance exchange across much of Eurasia under successor regimes.
- Khanates developed different political and cultural trajectories.

## Competing interpretation

Neither 'invincible horse archers' nor 'pure terror' is sufficient; organization, intelligence, opponent fragmentation, logistics, and adaptation matter.

## Counterfactual

More coordinated enemy coalitions and denial of forage would reduce the Mongol mobility advantage, as some later campaigns showed.
