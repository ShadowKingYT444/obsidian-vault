# Information Theory and Digital Communications

> Once information and channel capacity were formalized independently of message meaning, engineers could reason about compression and reliable communication as mathematical limit problems, enabling decades of coding and network design.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Telephony, radio, radar, and telegraph systems had created large noisy communication networks.
- Digital switching and electronic computing made discrete representations increasingly practical.

### Enabling conditions
- Probability theory supplied a language for uncertainty.
- Bell Labs linked theoretical researchers to real communication systems.

## Turning points
- Shannon's 1948 theory defined entropy and channel capacity.
- Error-correcting code theory developed practical approaches toward Shannon limits.
- Integrated circuits and digital signal processing made sophisticated coding economical.
- Internet and mobile systems embedded compression/error correction throughout daily infrastructure.

## Why it changed the system
- Communication design shifted from ad hoc signal handling toward formal tradeoffs among rate, noise, redundancy, and bandwidth.
- Information-theory vocabulary spread far beyond engineering, sometimes without its assumptions.

## Competing interpretation

Shannon supplied a foundational abstraction, but decades of mathematicians, chip designers, network engineers, and standards bodies converted limits into systems.

## Counterfactual

Without cheap digital computation, information theory remains intellectually important but many near-capacity schemes are impractical.
