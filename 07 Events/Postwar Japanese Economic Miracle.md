# Postwar Japanese Economic Miracle

> Japan's postwar growth combined reconstruction, education, high investment, technology import, quality improvement, export discipline, and global demand; no single industrial-policy institution explains the outcome.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Prewar Japan already had substantial industrial and technical capacity despite wartime destruction.
- Land reform, demobilization, and occupation-era institutional changes altered postwar incentives.

### Enabling conditions
- US security umbrella reduced defense burden and the Korean War boosted demand.
- High savings and bank-centered finance supported investment; firms adapted imported technologies.

## Turning points
- 1950s reconstruction shifted into high-growth industrial expansion.
- Quality-control and manufacturing practices improved export competitiveness.
- Oil shocks forced efficiency and sectoral adjustment rather than ending growth.
- The late-1980s asset bubble exposed weaknesses in finance and land markets.

## Why it changed the system
- Japan became a major automobile, electronics, machinery, and capital exporter.
- Later stagnation showed that institutions optimized for catch-up can face different problems near the productivity frontier.

## Competing interpretation

MITI-centered stories overstate central planning; firms, labor relations, finance, global markets, exchange rates, and US policy all mattered.

## Counterfactual

Without access to US markets and security, catch-up remains possible but resource allocation and export scale become much harder.
