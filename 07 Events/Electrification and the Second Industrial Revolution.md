# Electrification and the Second Industrial Revolution

> Electricity raised productivity only after generators, grids, motors, standards, factories, appliances, finance, and regulation co-evolved; early adoption often failed to gain much when firms kept old steam-era layouts.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Urbanization and industrial scale increased demand for light and flexible power.
- Advances in electromagnetism and machine engineering made practical generators/motors possible.

### Enabling conditions
- Copper, turbines, meters, transformers, and utility finance formed complementary infrastructure.
- Standard frequencies and voltages reduced coordination cost.

## Turning points
- Central stations created commercial lighting networks.
- AC transformation enabled larger transmission systems.
- Individual electric motors eventually let factories abandon central line shafts and redesign workflow.

## Why it changed the system
- Factories, homes, transport, communications, and urban life changed over decades.
- Utilities became regulated natural-monopoly-like infrastructure in many places.

## Competing interpretation

The invention date of a bulb or motor is less important economically than diffusion and complementary organizational redesign.

## Counterfactual

Simply replacing a steam engine with one electric motor while keeping the same factory layout captures only a fraction of electrification's potential.
