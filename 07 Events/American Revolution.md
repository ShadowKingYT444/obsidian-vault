# American Revolution

> American independence emerged from imperial fiscal conflict, colonial political mobilization, military survival, and decisive foreign intervention; the Revolution created republican institutions while preserving major exclusions.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Britain's post–Seven Years War debt increased pressure to tax and reorganize empire.
- Colonial assemblies had strong traditions of local political participation and disputed Parliament's authority to tax them.

### Enabling conditions
- Print networks and committees coordinated resistance across colonies.
- Geography made British occupation expensive; Atlantic rivals gave rebels access to foreign finance, weapons, and later military support.

## Turning points
- Lexington and Concord converted political conflict into war.
- The Declaration made independence the formal objective.
- Saratoga helped bring France into an alliance.
- French naval power at Yorktown blocked British relief and enabled decisive surrender.

## Why it changed the system
- The United States gained independence and later built a stronger federal constitution.
- Revolutionary liberty coexisted with slavery, Indigenous dispossession, limited suffrage, and Loyalist exile.

## Competing interpretation

A purely national story underweights French, Spanish, Dutch, Indigenous, enslaved, and imperial dimensions.

## Counterfactual

Without French intervention, the rebellion may persist, but forcing Britain to accept independence becomes much harder.
