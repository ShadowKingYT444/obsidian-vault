# Printing Revolution

> Movable-type printing reduced reproduction cost, increased textual standardization and scale, and created new markets and institutions; its impact depended on literacy, paper, distribution, censorship, and demand.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- European urban literacy and manuscript markets already existed.
- Paper production had lowered material cost before Gutenberg.

### Enabling conditions
- Movable metal type, presses, inks, financing, and bookseller networks worked as a system.
- Universities, churches, courts, and merchants created concentrated demand.

## Turning points
- Gutenberg's mid-fifteenth-century printing system demonstrated high-quality repeatable production.
- Printers spread rapidly across European cities.
- Reformation controversy created enormous demand for pamphlets, vernacular scripture, and polemic.

## Why it changed the system
- Texts became cheaper and more standardized; authors and readers could participate in larger discourse networks.
- Censorship and propaganda scaled alongside scholarship and science.

## Competing interpretation

Print did not mechanically create modernity; it changed information economics inside societies already changing in literacy and institutions.

## Counterfactual

Without cheap paper and distribution networks, movable type alone would have much smaller effects.
