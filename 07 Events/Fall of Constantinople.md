# Fall of Constantinople

> Ottoman victory in 1453 resulted from long-term Byzantine contraction, Ottoman state growth, siege logistics, artillery, and control of the surrounding region—not one giant cannon or one heroic assault.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- The Byzantine Empire had been territorially and fiscally weakened for centuries.
- The Ottoman state surrounded Constantinople and controlled much of its hinterland.

### Enabling conditions
- Mehmed II assembled artillery, ships, troops, and siege works at scale.
- European relief was limited by political division and other commitments.

## Turning points
- Ottoman control of the Bosporus reduced resupply.
- Sustained bombardment and assault wore down defenses.
- The final assault on 29 May breached the city's defensive system.

## Why it changed the system
- Constantinople became the Ottoman capital and a major imperial center.
- The event intensified European attention to Ottoman power, though claims that it alone 'caused the Renaissance' are false.

## Competing interpretation

Technology mattered, but artillery worked because a state could finance, move, supply, and protect it.

## Counterfactual

A larger coordinated relief fleet could prolong the siege, but Byzantine long-run strategic position remained extremely weak.
