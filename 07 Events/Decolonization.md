# Decolonization

> European empires unraveled because anticolonial organization, wartime exhaustion, changing legitimacy, international pressure, and the rising cost of coercion interacted; independence did not erase colonial borders or institutions.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- World wars weakened European finances and military prestige.
- Colonial education, urbanization, labor movements, veterans, and political organizations expanded anticolonial leadership.

### Enabling conditions
- UN norms and superpower competition made formal empire harder to legitimize.
- Different colonies had different settler populations, military importance, resources, and administrative depth.

## Turning points
- India/Pakistan independence in 1947 showed both rapid transfer and catastrophic partition violence.
- Indonesia, Vietnam, Algeria, Kenya, and others demonstrated that some empires resisted militarily.
- The 1960s brought a large wave of African independence.

## Why it changed the system
- Dozens of new states entered the international system.
- Many inherited borders, commodity dependence, administrative practices, and security institutions designed for colonial rule.

## Competing interpretation

Decolonization was not simply a gift from exhausted empires or an automatic result of nationalism; local struggle and imperial calculation both matter.

## Counterfactual

Earlier negotiated power-sharing might reduce violence in some colonies, but settler interests and racial hierarchies often blocked credible reform.
