# Crimean War and Sanitary Reform

> The Crimean War exposed how an army can lose more capability to disease, supply failure, and administration than to combat; the scandal accelerated hospital, statistical, and military-logistics reform.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- European powers contested Russian expansion and Ottoman decline.
- British military administration had weak peacetime logistics for an expeditionary war.

### Enabling conditions
- The telegraph and press transmitted reports of suffering rapidly to the public.
- Sanitary reform ideas and statistical methods already had advocates.

## Turning points
- The siege of Sevastopol became a prolonged logistical operation.
- Hospital mortality and supply failures caused public scandal.
- Nightingale and broader sanitary commissions improved reporting and sanitation; later reforms institutionalized lessons.

## Why it changed the system
- Military medicine and nursing professionalization accelerated.
- Public statistics became a stronger tool for administrative accountability.

## Competing interpretation

Nightingale mattered, but the mortality decline involved multiple commissions, engineers, doctors, supply reforms, and timing.

## Counterfactual

A competent prewar logistics and sanitation system could preserve large amounts of combat power without changing battlefield tactics.
