# Peloponnesian War

> Athens and Sparta fought inside alliance systems whose resources, political structures, and strategic asymmetries made a short decisive war difficult; plague, coalition shifts, and Athenian overreach changed the balance.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Athenian maritime empire and tribute generated fear and resentment among Sparta and allies.
- Sparta dominated land warfare while Athens depended on fleet, walls, trade, and imperial revenue.

### Enabling conditions
- The Delian and Peloponnesian leagues turned bilateral disputes into alliance commitments.
- Greek interstate norms offered no institution strong enough to absorb repeated security crises.

## Turning points
- The plague killed a large share of Athens' population and Pericles, weakening the original strategy.
- The Sicilian Expedition destroyed major Athenian forces far from the core theater.
- Persian finance later helped Sparta build a fleet capable of challenging Athenian sea power.

## Why it changed the system
- Athens lost its empire and democratic stability was briefly disrupted.
- The war weakened major Greek poleis and created space later exploited by Macedon.

## Competing interpretation

Thucydides emphasizes fear, honor, and interest; modern readings debate whether war was structurally inevitable or produced by avoidable crisis decisions.

## Counterfactual

If Athens avoids Sicily, Sparta still faces a hard problem: how to defeat a fortified maritime empire without Persian naval finance.
