# Russian Revolution

> War destroyed the legitimacy and operating capacity of the tsarist state; the resulting power vacuum allowed competing institutions to emerge, and the Bolsheviks converted a disciplined party plus control of strategic nodes into state power during continuing social breakdown.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Autocracy faced land hunger, labor unrest, national tensions, and weak representative institutions.
- World War I produced military losses, inflation, transport failure, and food-distribution crisis.

### Enabling conditions
- Workers' and soldiers' soviets provided alternate centers of authority.
- The Provisional Government inherited war commitments without stable coercive control.

## Turning points
- The February Revolution removed Nicholas II after mass protest and military defection.
- Dual power limited the Provisional Government's authority.
- The Bolsheviks took key institutions in the October Revolution.
- Civil war and War Communism centralized coercive institutions further.

## Why it changed the system
- The Soviet state emerged and reshaped global politics for the twentieth century.
- Civil war, famine, repression, and economic collapse caused enormous human costs.

## Competing interpretation

Bolshevik organization mattered, but it operated inside a state collapse created by war, social conflict, and failures of competing governments.

## Counterfactual

An earlier Russian exit from World War I could reduce state breakdown, though land and legitimacy crises would remain.
