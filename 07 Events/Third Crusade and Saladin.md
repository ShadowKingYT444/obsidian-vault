# Third Crusade and Saladin

> Jerusalem's recovery by Saladin followed years of coalition consolidation and Crusader strategic error; the Third Crusade then showed the limits of battlefield success when neither side could cheaply eliminate the other's political base.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Crusader states depended on fortified nodes, maritime support, and divided regional politics.
- Saladin's control of Egypt and Syria created a stronger resource coalition than earlier Muslim fragmentation.

### Enabling conditions
- Religious legitimacy helped mobilization on both sides.
- European maritime powers and royal expeditions could reinforce coastal Crusader positions.

## Turning points
- At Hattin in 1187, the Crusader army was drawn away from secure water and destroyed.
- Jerusalem surrendered to Saladin later that year.
- Richard I won important coastal battles but could not secure Jerusalem at acceptable operational risk.

## Why it changed the system
- The treaty preserved a reduced Crusader coastal presence while Jerusalem remained under Muslim rule.
- Saladin's coalition fragmented among Ayyubid relatives after his death.

## Competing interpretation

Chivalric hero stories on both sides can obscure logistics, coalition politics, fortification, and finance.

## Counterfactual

If the Crusader army avoids Hattin's operational trap, Saladin faces a much slower fortress-and-coalition problem.
