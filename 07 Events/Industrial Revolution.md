# Industrial Revolution

> Industrialization was a system transition: cheap energy, machinery, capital, skilled labor, transport, institutions, and expanding markets reinforced each other; no single invention explains the takeoff.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- Britain had high wages relative to coal/energy costs in important sectors, commercial institutions, and expanding markets.
- Agricultural and demographic changes increased labor mobility and demand.

### Enabling conditions
- Coal, steam power, machine tools, textile machinery, canals, and later railways formed complementary technologies.
- Finance, patents, craft skill, and imperial/global trade provided capital and markets, alongside coercive colonial structures.

## Turning points
- Mechanized spinning and weaving transformed textiles.
- Improved steam engines expanded reliable power beyond water sites.
- Railways reduced transport cost and created demand for coal, iron, engineering, and standardized time.

## Why it changed the system
- Output and urbanization rose dramatically while factory labor produced harsh conditions and new class politics.
- Industrial military and transport capacity reshaped global power.

## Competing interpretation

'Why Britain first?' remains contested across wages, coal, institutions, empire, science, culture, and global trade; monocausal answers are weak.

## Counterfactual

A steam engine without cheap coal, machine-building skill, capital, and demand remains a niche technology rather than an industrial system.
