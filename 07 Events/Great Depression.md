# Great Depression

> The Depression became global and persistent because financial collapse interacted with debt, banking fragility, gold-standard constraints, deflation, falling demand, and policy errors; recovery paths differed with institutions and timing.

## Causal stack

### Structural pressures
- The interwar monetary system linked countries through the gold standard and war-debt/reparation structures.
- US banking was fragmented and vulnerable to repeated panics.

### Enabling conditions
- High leverage and asset-price optimism amplified the late-1920s boom.
- Limited deposit insurance and lender-of-last-resort practice made bank runs destructive.

## Turning points
- The 1929 crash damaged wealth and confidence but was not alone sufficient to explain the depression's depth.
- Banking crises and monetary contraction sharply reduced credit and money supply.
- Countries leaving gold gained more room for monetary expansion.
- US New Deal reforms changed banking, labor, welfare, and regulatory institutions.

## Why it changed the system
- Mass unemployment changed political coalitions and confidence in laissez-faire policy.
- Economic collapse contributed to authoritarian politics and international instability, though outcomes varied by country.

## Competing interpretation

Debate centers on monetary contraction, demand, banking, gold, debt-deflation, and policy; the event is a network of reinforcing mechanisms rather than one mistake.

## Counterfactual

Earlier aggressive lender-of-last-resort action and departure from deflationary gold constraints likely reduce severity, though exact paths differ.
