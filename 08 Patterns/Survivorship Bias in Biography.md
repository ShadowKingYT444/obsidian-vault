# Survivorship Bias in Biography

> If you study only famous winners, traits common among winners can look causal even when they were also common among forgotten failures.

## Mechanism

- Historical attention samples on the outcome.
- Vivid anecdotes overweight unusual personalities.
- Failed imitators leave fewer archives and biographies.

## Cases

- Extreme work hours, confidence, risk taking, and stubbornness appear in success stories and failures.
- Military audacity looks brilliant when it succeeds and reckless when it fails; outcome bias changes the label.
- Founder biographies often under-sample timing and luck.

## Boundary conditions / failure cases

- Selection bias does not make traits irrelevant; it means you need comparison groups and mechanisms.
- Some repeated traits survive stronger cross-case testing.

## Retrieval and transfer questions

- Who had the same trait and failed?
- What base rate would change the lesson?
