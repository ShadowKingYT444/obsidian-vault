# Coalition Management

> Many strategic outcomes depend on holding together actors whose interests overlap only partially.

## Mechanism

- Coalitions aggregate resources no member controls alone.
- Side payments, status, commitments, and shared threats stabilize cooperation.
- Internal fracture can matter more than direct opponent strength.

## Cases

- Rome's Italian alliance system helped it absorb defeats during the Second Punic War.
- Anti-Napoleonic coalitions improved as members learned to coordinate and persist after defeats.
- Political reform often packages policies so different groups gain different reasons to support them.

## Boundary conditions / failure cases

- Coalitions slow decisions and create veto points.
- A shared enemy can disappear after victory and expose conflict.

## Retrieval and transfer questions

- What keeps each member in?
- Which member is pivotal enough to demand disproportionate concessions?
