# Great Man Theory vs Structural History

> Individuals can redirect history, but the size of their effect depends on institutions, technology, coalitions, and resources.

## Mechanism

- Agency explains choice among feasible paths; structure defines much of the feasible set.
- Crises can increase individual leverage because normal constraints weaken.
- Strong institutions can reduce dependence on one person.

## Cases

- Caesar accelerated the Roman Republic's collapse, but elite competition, military patronage, and provincial wealth preceded him.
- Napoleon transformed French power, but mass conscription and revolutionary institutions enlarged the instrument available to him.
- Deng Xiaoping mattered, but reform also used local experimentation, human capital, diaspora links, and global demand.

## Boundary conditions / failure cases

- Structural explanations can become so broad that they stop predicting choices.
- Personality matters most at forks where several feasible choices have different consequences.

## Retrieval and transfer questions

- If this person vanished five years earlier, what likely still happens?
- Which outcome required this person's specific choice?
