# Compounding and Learning Curves

> Repeated production and experimentation can lower cost and error because knowledge accumulates in tools, procedures, people, and designs.

## Mechanism

- Workers learn sequence and failure modes.
- Designs absorb manufacturing feedback.
- Scale justifies specialized equipment and suppliers.

## Cases

- Mass production reduced unit cost through standardization and process learning.
- Aircraft and semiconductor industries show experience effects, though rates vary.
- Scientific communities compound through shared instruments, methods, and results.

## Boundary conditions / failure cases

- Learning can plateau.
- Volume without measurement repeats mistakes rather than learning.
- Rapid design churn can reset process knowledge.

## Retrieval and transfer questions

- Where is learning stored?
- Does doubling cumulative output improve the bottleneck?
