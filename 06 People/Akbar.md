# Akbar

> A case in imperial scaling through elite incorporation, revenue reform, negotiated diversity, and a legitimacy model broader than narrow dynastic conquest.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Expand and stabilize Mughal rule across a large, religiously and politically diverse population.
- **Constraints:** Regional rulers, revenue complexity, military aristocracy, communication distance, and religious diversity.

- Integrated many Rajput elites through alliance, service, and marriage politics.
- Developed mansabdari rank/service structures and supported revenue reforms associated with Todar Mal.
- Used court ritual and religious debate to strengthen a supra-sectarian imperial identity.

## Decisions to study

1. Rajput accommodation reduced the need for permanent coercion in important regions.
2. Abolishing the jizya during his reign signaled a broader political inclusion strategy.
3. Central ranking and revenue systems linked military service more directly to imperial administration.

## Failure modes and costs

- Imperial tolerance was politically constructed and did not remove coercion or hierarchy.
- Some ideological experiments, such as Din-i Ilahi, had very limited uptake and are often exaggerated.

## Context you cannot copy

Akbar inherited Timurid-Mughal military traditions and a state rebuilt by Humayun and key regents.
