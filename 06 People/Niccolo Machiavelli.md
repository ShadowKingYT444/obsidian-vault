# Niccolo Machiavelli

> A case in analyzing political action under conflict and uncertainty without assuming that moral intention automatically produces political survival.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Understand how republics and rulers acquire, preserve, and lose power.
- **Constraints:** Factional Italian politics, foreign invasions, personal exile, and dependence on ancient historical examples.

- Separated effective political means from idealized moral self-description.
- Compared Roman republican institutions with contemporary Italian weakness.
- Emphasized adaptation to fortune: the same behavior can succeed in one environment and fail in another.

## Decisions to study

1. The Prince compressed lessons for a ruler in a crisis context.
2. Discourses on Livy gave a broader and often more republican account of institutions and conflict.
3. His Florentine militia project reflected distrust of mercenary dependence.

## Failure modes and costs

- Reading only The Prince creates a distorted picture of his political thought.
- Historical analogies can fail when institutions and military technologies differ.

## Context you cannot copy

Italy was divided among competing states and repeatedly intervened in by larger powers.
