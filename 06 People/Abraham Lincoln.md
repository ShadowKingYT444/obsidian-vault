# Abraham Lincoln

> A case in aligning political purpose, military strategy, coalition management, and public communication during a civil war whose goals changed as the conflict evolved.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Preserve the Union, then link Union victory to emancipation while maintaining a governing coalition capable of sustaining war.
- **Constraints:** Constitutional uncertainty, border-state politics, military setbacks, elections, racism, and enormous casualties.

- Repeatedly reframed policy in language broad enough to hold different Union constituencies.
- Changed generals until finding commanders who could translate material superiority into coordinated pressure.
- Used emancipation as a moral, military, diplomatic, and political instrument, while the actions of enslaved people themselves also drove events.

## Decisions to study

1. Suspending habeas corpus in parts of the country showed the civil-liberties cost of emergency rule.
2. The Emancipation Proclamation changed the war's political character within its legal/military scope.
3. Backing Grant in 1864 accepted high short-term casualties for sustained strategic pressure.

## Failure modes and costs

- Some emergency actions remain deeply contested.
- Lincoln's racial views evolved but did not match modern equality standards, and emancipation was not simply a top-down presidential gift.

## Context you cannot copy

Northern population, industry, rail, finance, Black military participation, and Confederate choices strongly shaped the feasible strategy.
