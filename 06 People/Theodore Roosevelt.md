# Theodore Roosevelt

> A case in agenda setting and executive energy: he expanded the presidency's role by treating public opinion, expert administration, and visible intervention as tools of governance.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Make federal power more capable of arbitrating industrial conflict, regulating concentrated corporate power, conserving resources, and projecting US influence.
- **Constraints:** Courts, Congress, party factions, corporate power, racial hierarchy, and constitutional norms.

- Used the presidency as a 'bully pulpit' to frame issues and mobilize public pressure.
- Distinguished between acceptable and abusive large corporations rather than opposing scale itself.
- Expanded expert administration in conservation and regulation.

## Decisions to study

1. Intervening in the 1902 coal strike challenged a purely hands-off federal posture.
2. Northern Securities prosecution signaled antitrust enforcement against concentrated rail power.
3. Panama Canal policy showed strategic ambition but also coercive US behavior toward weaker states.

## Failure modes and costs

- His imperial and racial views included serious injustices and hierarchy.
- Executive activism can weaken restraint if the leader's goals are worse than Roosevelt's preferred reforms.

## Context you cannot copy

Industrial concentration, mass journalism, Progressive reform networks, and growing US power created opportunities.
