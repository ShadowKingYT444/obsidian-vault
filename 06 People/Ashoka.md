# Ashoka

> A case in imperial communication and moral legitimation: conquest was followed by an effort to define rule through publicly inscribed ethical claims.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Maintain a large Mauryan empire while presenting kingship as morally disciplined after the Kalinga war.
- **Constraints:** Vast geography, diverse communities, limited transport/communication, and dependence on provincial administration.

- Used rock and pillar edicts to broadcast policy and royal ethics across regions.
- Promoted dhamma as a broad civic-moral framework rather than a narrow sectarian code.
- Combined welfare measures, officials, and symbolic patronage with inherited imperial structures.

## Decisions to study

1. The Kalinga war became central to his public rejection of further conquest by violence.
2. Edicts addressed officials and subjects directly across multiple languages and scripts.
3. Support for Buddhist institutions helped later memory of Ashoka, but his imperial policy was broader than Buddhism alone.

## Failure modes and costs

- Later Buddhist traditions can obscure the political and administrative dimensions of his reign.
- The Mauryan Empire weakened after his death; moral messaging did not solve succession and structural problems.

## Context you cannot copy

Ashoka inherited an already large empire built by Chandragupta and Bindusara.
