# Saladin

> A case in patient coalition building: his greatest strategic problem was first unifying enough Muslim political power to make victory over the Crusader states feasible.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Consolidate Egypt and Syria, legitimize Ayyubid rule, and recover Jerusalem from the Crusader states.
- **Constraints:** Rival Muslim rulers, dynastic politics, fiscal needs, Crusader fortresses, and the need for religious legitimacy.

- Used diplomacy, kinship, appointments, and military pressure to assemble a broader coalition.
- Converted control of Egypt into a fiscal base for campaigns in Syria and Palestine.
- Used jihad rhetoric as one legitimacy layer while practicing pragmatic diplomacy and truces.

## Decisions to study

1. Prioritized Muslim political consolidation before full confrontation with the Crusader states.
2. Exploited Crusader operational mistakes before the Battle of Hattin in 1187.
3. After taking Jerusalem, negotiated surrender rather than reproducing the massacre associated with 1099.

## Failure modes and costs

- His coalition was personal and dynastic; after his death Ayyubid territories were divided among relatives.
- Later reputation can romanticize chivalry and understate hard political bargaining.

## Context you cannot copy

He inherited institutions and opportunities from Nur al-Din's state and Fatimid Egypt's resources.
