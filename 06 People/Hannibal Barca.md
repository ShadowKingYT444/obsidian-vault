# Hannibal Barca

> A reminder that repeated tactical brilliance can fail strategically when the opponent's alliance system, manpower, and political will survive.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Break Roman power by invading Italy and causing Rome's allies to defect.
- **Constraints:** Distance from Carthage, limited siege capacity, uncertain reinforcement, and Rome's deep manpower coalition.

- Created local tactical asymmetry through deception, terrain, and combined arms.
- Turned an unexpected Alpine approach into strategic surprise.
- Tried to convert battlefield victories into political defections among Rome's allies.

## Decisions to study

1. The Alpine invasion created a theater Rome did not expect.
2. Cannae maximized tactical destruction but did not destroy Rome's alliance network.
3. Remaining in Italy without decisive siege or political collapse exposed the gap between battle and strategy.

## Failure modes and costs

- Could not force Rome to negotiate after Cannae.
- Carthaginian political and logistical support was not sufficient to match Rome's replacement capacity.

## Context you cannot copy

Rome's federated Italian system and willingness to raise new armies made ordinary victory conditions unusually hard to achieve.
