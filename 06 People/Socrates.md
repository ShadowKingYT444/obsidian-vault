# Socrates

> Useful less as a doctrine-builder than as a model of adversarial questioning: expose hidden definitions, contradictions, and unjustified certainty.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Examine claims about virtue, knowledge, justice, and the good life through dialogue.
- **Constraints:** Oral teaching, politically charged postwar Athens, and sources written by students or later authors.

- Asked for definitions before accepting conclusions.
- Used counterexamples to force revision of broad claims.
- Separated confidence from knowledge by making interlocutors state reasons.

## Decisions to study

1. Refused to stop philosophizing when prosecuted.
2. Accepted the legal sentence rather than escaping, according to Plato's account.
3. Kept inquiry personal and conversational rather than building a written system.

## Failure modes and costs

- The method can dissolve weak claims without always supplying a positive theory.
- Our picture of Socrates is filtered heavily through Plato, Xenophon, Aristophanes, and later tradition.

## Context you cannot copy

His trial followed oligarchic coups and the Peloponnesian War; some associates had politically toxic reputations.
