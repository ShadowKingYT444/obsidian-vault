# Ulysses S Grant

> A case in operational persistence and system-level coordination: Grant increasingly treated enemy armies, geography, rail, rivers, and simultaneous theaters as one connected problem.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Destroy Confederate capacity to sustain war rather than merely capture symbolic territory.
- **Constraints:** Large theaters, political scrutiny, difficult logistics, high casualties, and strong Confederate field armies.

- Used rivers and maneuver to create logistical and positional advantages in the western theater.
- Accepted temporary tactical setbacks while preserving strategic initiative.
- As general-in-chief, coordinated pressure across theaters to reduce Confederate ability to shift reserves.

## Decisions to study

1. The Vicksburg campaign crossed the Mississippi and operated temporarily from an unusual supply posture to create operational freedom.
2. After the Wilderness, moving south rather than retreating signaled sustained pressure.
3. Appomattox surrender terms were designed to end organized resistance without unnecessary humiliation.

## Failure modes and costs

- The Overland Campaign incurred enormous casualties and remains debated in tactical terms.
- As president, Grant's administration suffered from corruption among associates despite his own reputation for personal honesty.

## Context you cannot copy

Union material superiority mattered only when converted into continuous coordinated operations; Grant did not create those resources.
