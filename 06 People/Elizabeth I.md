# Elizabeth I

> A case in governing through ambiguity, faction balance, symbolic legitimacy, and selective commitment rather than constant maximal action.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Secure a contested Protestant monarchy while avoiding domestic fracture and ruinous continental war.
- **Constraints:** Religious division, disputed succession, limited finances, powerful European rivals, and gendered legitimacy challenges.

- Maintained access to competing court factions without letting one permanently monopolize policy.
- Used royal image, progresses, speeches, and controlled marriage ambiguity as political instruments.
- Often delayed irreversible commitments until external and domestic conditions clarified.

## Decisions to study

1. The Elizabethan Religious Settlement sought enough doctrinal flexibility for political stability.
2. Long refusal to marry preserved diplomatic options but created succession uncertainty.
3. Support for the Dutch and privateering escalated conflict with Spain while trying to control direct costs.

## Failure modes and costs

- Late-war finances and Ireland imposed severe strain.
- Her avoidance of succession planning shifted risk into the end of the reign.

## Context you cannot copy

She inherited Tudor administrative development, Reformation conflict, and a strong council tradition.
