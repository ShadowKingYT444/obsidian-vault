# Catherine the Great

> A case in reform constrained by elite dependence: an intellectually ambitious ruler expanded state power while relying on the social order she sometimes criticized.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Secure a fragile throne, modernize parts of administration and culture, and expand Russian geopolitical power.
- **Constraints:** Dependence on nobility, serfdom, vast geography, court politics, and repeated war.

- Cultivated elite support through patronage and charters while centralizing prestige around the crown.
- Used Enlightenment language and legal commissions to frame reform.
- Expanded south and west through war and diplomacy, including partitions of Poland.

## Decisions to study

1. The Nakaz presented reformist legal principles without producing a full code.
2. Response to Pugachev's rebellion reinforced reliance on noble control of the countryside.
3. Territorial expansion increased Russian power but also imperial commitments.

## Failure modes and costs

- Serfdom deepened in important respects despite enlightened rhetoric.
- Court favoritism and elite bargains limited the reach of universal reform claims.

## Context you cannot copy

She came to power through a coup and therefore needed constant elite coalition management.
