# George Washington

> A case in strategic survival and institutional restraint: preserving a weak army and maintaining political legitimacy mattered more than winning every battle.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Keep the American rebellion militarily viable long enough for British political costs and foreign support to make independence possible.
- **Constraints:** Short enlistments, weak finance, supply shortages, divided colonies, stronger British conventional forces.

- Avoided making army destruction the price of symbolic battlefield victory.
- Maintained civilian supremacy despite military frustration.
- Used personal reputation at key moments to stabilize fragile institutions.

## Decisions to study

1. Retreat after New York preserved the Continental Army despite political embarrassment.
2. Trenton and Princeton restored morale with bounded offensive risk.
3. Resigning his commission in 1783 and later leaving the presidency after two terms strengthened norms against military or personal rule.

## Failure modes and costs

- Early conventional battles exposed inexperience.
- He was a slaveholder; political liberty coexisted with personal participation in slavery, a central moral and institutional contradiction.

## Context you cannot copy

French military/naval intervention, local militias, Atlantic finance, and British strategic priorities were essential to victory.
