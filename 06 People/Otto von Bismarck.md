# Otto von Bismarck

> A case in bounded strategic aims, alliance engineering, and sequencing: Bismarck used limited wars to alter the political map, then shifted toward preserving the new equilibrium.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Increase Prussian power and unify Germany under Prussian leadership without triggering a durable hostile coalition.
- **Constraints:** Austria, France, Russia, smaller German states, domestic constitutional conflict, and fear of encirclement.

- Sequenced diplomatic isolation and limited wars rather than fighting all rivals at once.
- Used nationalism while keeping dynastic and conservative institutions intact.
- After unification, built alliances intended to make Germany a satisfied central power rather than a revisionist one.

## Decisions to study

1. The 1866 war with Austria was kept limited; Bismarck resisted demands for harsher peace.
2. The 1870 crisis with France helped bring southern German states into unification.
3. Later alliance diplomacy tried to prevent France from gaining strong partners.

## Failure modes and costs

- His domestic use of anti-Catholic and anti-socialist campaigns produced backlash and contradiction.
- The system depended heavily on complex personal diplomacy and was difficult for successors to maintain.

## Context you cannot copy

Prussian military reform, industrial growth, railways, nationalism, and Austrian structural problems were indispensable.
