# Thomas Edison

> A case in system invention: Edison's durable advantage was often not one device but an organized pipeline linking experimentation, components, patents, manufacturing, finance, and deployment.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Turn electrical and communications inventions into reliable, manufacturable systems with commercial markets.
- **Constraints:** Material limitations, uncertain demand, patent competition, capital needs, and infrastructure dependence.

- Built Menlo Park as an organized invention laboratory with specialized collaborators.
- Treated the electric light as a full system of generation, wiring, meters, lamps, and distribution.
- Ran large numbers of experiments and recorded results to search material/design spaces.

## Decisions to study

1. The incandescent-light program optimized a system rather than only a bulb.
2. DC distribution created an early commercial network but scaled poorly over distance compared with AC.
3. Edison used patents and publicity aggressively as competitive tools.

## Failure modes and costs

- The 'lone wizard' myth undercredits laboratory staff and prior inventors.
- His attachment to DC and public campaign against AC shows how incumbent architecture can bias technical judgment.

## Context you cannot copy

Telegraph networks, machine shops, patent law, Wall Street finance, and rapidly growing cities enabled his model.
