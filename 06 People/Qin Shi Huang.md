# Qin Shi Huang

> A high-intensity state-capacity case: standardization and centralized administration enabled unification, while coercive extraction made the dynasty brittle.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Destroy rival states and make political unification administratively real.
- **Constraints:** Recent conquest, regional variation, elite resistance, huge mobilization demands, and succession risk.

- Extended commanderies and counties instead of relying mainly on hereditary feudal rule.
- Standardized weights, measures, script forms, roads, and other administrative interfaces.
- Used Legalist institutions for taxation, labor mobilization, military service, and enforcement.

## Decisions to study

1. Completed conquest of the rival Warring States by 221 BCE.
2. Dismantled many aristocratic bases of local power.
3. Large construction and frontier projects increased state reach but also extraction burdens.

## Failure modes and costs

- The dynasty collapsed soon after his death amid succession manipulation, rebellion, and accumulated resentment.
- Coercive capacity was not the same as stable legitimacy.

## Context you cannot copy

Qin reforms predated the First Emperor; Shang Yang-era institutions and long interstate competition built the machine he inherited.
