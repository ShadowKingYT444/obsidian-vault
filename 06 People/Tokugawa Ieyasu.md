# Tokugawa Ieyasu

> A case in converting battlefield victory into a durable political settlement by controlling hostages, domains, marriage, infrastructure, and legitimacy.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Secure supremacy after decades of civil war and prevent rival daimyo from rebuilding independent military coalitions.
- **Constraints:** Powerful regional lords, fragile alliances, succession, and the memory of rapid regime turnover.

- Balanced domains and classified lords by political reliability.
- Used alternate attendance's precursors and later Tokugawa systems to make elite autonomy expensive.
- Combined military supremacy with court titles, law, marriage politics, and controlled succession.

## Decisions to study

1. Victory at Sekigahara in 1600 established military primacy.
2. Taking the shogunate in 1603 translated power into an institutional title.
3. Eliminating the Toyotomi at Osaka removed a focal rival before succession fully stabilized.

## Failure modes and costs

- The later Tokugawa order traded some adaptability for stability and status hierarchy.
- Some institutions credited to Ieyasu were developed further by successors, so founder-centric stories overstate one person's design.

## Context you cannot copy

Oda Nobunaga and Toyotomi Hideyoshi had already transformed warfare and centralized significant power.
