# Steve Jobs

> A case in integrated product judgment: Jobs repeatedly bet that controlling hardware, software, interface, and distribution could create a coherent user experience—but this depended on strong technical teams and platform timing.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Build computing products that hide technical complexity behind a coherent, emotionally legible interface.
- **Constraints:** Fast technology cycles, manufacturing economics, developer ecosystems, organizational politics, and the risk of closed platforms.

- Made product scope a strategic choice by killing many projects and concentrating resources.
- Insisted on end-to-end integration where interface quality depended on coordinated hardware/software decisions.
- Used launches and narrative to turn product architecture into a market category people could understand.

## Decisions to study

1. The Macintosh prioritized graphical interaction before the economics and market were fully mature.
2. Returning to Apple, Jobs simplified the product matrix and restored financial focus.
3. The iPhone removed the physical keyboard and combined phone, browser, and media device around a multi-touch software platform.

## Failure modes and costs

- His interpersonal style could be abusive and is not required for high standards.
- Closed integration can reduce flexibility and depends on ecosystem leverage and excellent internal execution.

## Context you cannot copy

Xerox PARC research, semiconductor scaling, global supply chains, design teams, NeXT software, and mobile networks were critical prerequisites.
