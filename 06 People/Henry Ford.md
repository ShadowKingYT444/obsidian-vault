# Henry Ford

> A case in process architecture: Ford made a product cheap by redesigning the factory, standardizing the product, and aligning labor policy with throughput—then struggled when variety and market preferences changed.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Produce reliable automobiles at a price reachable by a mass market.
- **Constraints:** Part variation, skilled-labor bottlenecks, worker turnover, supplier coordination, and capital-intensive factories.

- Standardized components and product design to make flow production possible.
- Moved work to the worker through assembly-line sequencing.
- Used high wages partly to reduce turnover and stabilize a demanding production system.

## Decisions to study

1. The Model T concentrated volume into a narrow product architecture.
2. The moving assembly line dramatically reduced assembly time when upstream processes matched it.
3. Long resistance to replacing the Model T showed the danger of optimizing around a once-dominant product.

## Failure modes and costs

- Management was authoritarian and labor relations became coercive and violent.
- Ford's antisemitic publications were harmful and influential; operational achievement does not excuse ideology.

## Context you cannot copy

Machine tools, interchangeable parts, steel, rubber, roads, oil, and rising consumer incomes were complementary systems.
