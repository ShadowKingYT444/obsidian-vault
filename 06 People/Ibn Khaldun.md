# Ibn Khaldun

> A powerful early model-builder who tried to explain dynastic rise and decline through group solidarity, fiscal behavior, luxury, and institutional change rather than isolated anecdotes.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Create a method for separating plausible historical explanation from unreliable chronicle and explain recurring political patterns.
- **Constraints:** Fragmented dynastic politics, uneven source quality, personal political entanglement, and premodern economic data.

- Used asabiyyah, or group solidarity, to explain how cohesive frontier groups can defeat wealthier but less cohesive states.
- Connected taxation, incentives, urban luxury, military dependence, and dynastic cycles.
- Criticized reports by checking them against social and economic possibility.

## Decisions to study

1. The Muqaddimah placed a theory of society before narrative history.
2. His political career across competing courts exposed him directly to elite instability.
3. He treated state power as a social process rather than only a sequence of rulers.

## Failure modes and costs

- His cyclical model can overgeneralize from North African and Islamic dynastic experience.
- Asabiyyah is useful but can become a catch-all if not operationalized carefully.

## Context you cannot copy

His theories emerged from repeated regime turnover, tribal politics, plague-era disruption, and his own administrative career.
