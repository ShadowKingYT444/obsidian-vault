# Nelson Mandela

> A case in shifting from resistance to negotiated transition while keeping enough coalition credibility to prevent compromise from becoming political collapse.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** End apartheid and establish majority-rule democracy without triggering a wider civil war or irreversible economic breakdown.
- **Constraints:** State repression, internal movement factions, racial fear, political violence, international pressure, and institutional inequality.

- Used personal symbolism to reassure groups that feared revenge while maintaining commitment to political equality.
- Supported negotiation when power relations changed enough to make a settlement possible.
- Shared transition leadership with a broader ANC organization rather than acting as a lone reconciler.

## Decisions to study

1. Secret and then formal negotiations opened before apartheid had fully collapsed.
2. Supporting the 1995 Rugby World Cup as a national symbol was a deliberate legitimacy gesture, not a substitute for policy.
3. Stepping down after one presidential term strengthened democratic succession norms.

## Failure modes and costs

- Reconciliation did not solve deep economic inequality or land distribution.
- Hero narratives can erase the roles of mass organizing, labor, international sanctions, armed resistance, and other leaders.

## Context you cannot copy

The ANC, civil society, global anti-apartheid pressure, business interests, and changes in the Cold War shaped the settlement space.
