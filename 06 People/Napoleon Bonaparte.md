# Napoleon Bonaparte

> A high-density study in operational speed, staff work, institutional consolidation, personal command, and the way a system optimized for decisive campaigns can fail under overextension and coalition adaptation.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** First survive revolutionary politics, then secure French dominance and a durable dynastic order.
- **Constraints:** Coalition warfare, mass armies, fiscal demands, legitimacy after revolution, and increasing geographic scale.

- Used corps-level operational autonomy to disperse for movement and concentrate for battle.
- Processed large volumes of administrative and military information through staff and correspondence.
- Preserved or rebuilt institutions—law, administration, education, finance—while concentrating executive power.

## Decisions to study

1. The 1805 Ulm-Austerlitz campaign exploited tempo, deception, and separated enemy forces.
2. The Continental System tried to turn land dominance into economic coercion against Britain.
3. The 1812 invasion of Russia made distance, supply, climate, attrition, and political goals exceed the system's earlier operating envelope.

## Failure modes and costs

- Repeated victories encouraged escalating commitments and reduced willingness to accept bounded settlements.
- Centralization around his personal judgment created exceptional coherence but succession and delegation limits.

## Context you cannot copy

Revolutionary France supplied mass mobilization, promoted talent, reorganized administration, and weakened old European assumptions before Napoleon's rise.
