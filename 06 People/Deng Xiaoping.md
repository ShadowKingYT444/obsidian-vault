# Deng Xiaoping

> A case in pragmatic institutional experimentation: decentralize some economic decisions, test reforms locally, keep political control centralized, then scale what appears to work.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Raise productivity and state strength after Mao-era disruption without losing Communist Party rule.
- **Constraints:** Ideological legacy, weak incentives, rural poverty, bureaucratic resistance, political risk, and fear of instability.

- Allowed local and sectoral experiments before full national commitment.
- Changed incentives in agriculture, township industry, trade, and special economic zones.
- Used ambiguous slogans to make policy change politically legible without requiring total ideological repudiation.

## Decisions to study

1. Household responsibility reforms weakened collective agriculture in practice and improved farm incentives.
2. Special economic zones concentrated openness where outcomes could be observed.
3. The 1989 Tiananmen crackdown demonstrated the hard boundary placed around political pluralization.

## Failure modes and costs

- Rapid growth came with corruption, inequality, environmental costs, and political repression.
- Pragmatic flexibility can become difficult to audit when official doctrine and actual practice diverge.

## Context you cannot copy

Reform benefited from prior investments in health/education, a strong party-state, overseas Chinese capital, East Asian growth networks, and expanding global trade.
