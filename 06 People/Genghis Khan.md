# Genghis Khan

> A case in turning fragmented steppe networks into a scalable military-political system through coalition building, disciplined organization, mobility, and selective incorporation of expertise.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Unify rival steppe groups, defeat external enemies, and maintain loyalty across a rapidly expanding confederation.
- **Constraints:** Tribal rivalry, sparse population, vast distances, succession risk, and dependence on mobility.

- Reorganized forces through decimal units that weakened some old tribal loyalties and improved command.
- Promoted capable commanders beyond narrow aristocratic lineage, while still relying on personal and family bonds.
- Absorbed engineers, administrators, merchants, and intelligence from conquered societies.

## Decisions to study

1. Breaking rival coalitions in Mongolia before large external conquests created a stronger base.
2. Using terror selectively could induce surrender but also produced enormous human costs.
3. Delegating major operations to trusted commanders allowed simultaneous campaigns over great distances.

## Failure modes and costs

- Conquests caused mass killing and destruction in several regions.
- The imperial succession system eventually divided authority among branches of the ruling family.

## Context you cannot copy

Steppe cavalry ecology, horse availability, existing nomadic institutions, and fragmented opponents were essential conditions.
