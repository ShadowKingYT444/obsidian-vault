# Alexander the Great

> A case in operational tempo, personal command, coalition inheritance, and the fragility of conquest without a durable succession system.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Break Persian power and build a universal monarchy around Macedonian military dominance.
- **Constraints:** A small Macedonian core, long supply lines, mixed allied forces, and the need to govern conquered elites.

- Inherited Philip II's reformed army and staff system rather than creating capability from zero.
- Used speed, combined arms, and repeated concentration at decisive points.
- Co-opted Persian elites and symbols to extend rule beyond Macedonian manpower.

## Decisions to study

1. Crossing into Asia in 334 BCE with limited cash but a high-tempo strategy.
2. Forcing decisive battles at Issus and Gaugamela rather than settling for coastal gains.
3. Continuing east after Persia's collapse, which expanded prestige and strain at the same time.

## Failure modes and costs

- No stable succession arrangement existed at his death; the empire fragmented among successors.
- Escalating personal rule and cultural fusion generated resistance among some Macedonian officers.

## Context you cannot copy

His system depended on Philip's institutions, elite cavalry, siege capability, and an opponent with a specific imperial structure.
