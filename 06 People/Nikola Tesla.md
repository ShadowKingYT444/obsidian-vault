# Nikola Tesla

> A case in powerful technical imagination whose best ideas succeeded when paired with complementary capital, manufacturing, and deployment systems.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Develop practical alternating-current motors, power systems, and high-frequency electrical technologies.
- **Constraints:** Capital dependence, patent competition, commercialization skill, and the gap between physical possibility and economic system design.

- Used rotating magnetic-field concepts to build polyphase induction motors.
- Thought in complete electromechanical systems and vivid internal spatial models.
- Relied on licensing/partnerships, especially with Westinghouse, for broad deployment.

## Decisions to study

1. AC motor and polyphase patents aligned with long-distance transmission advantages.
2. The Westinghouse relationship converted inventions into an industrial system.
3. Later wireless-power projects pursued very ambitious goals without a stable business model or demonstrated end-to-end economics.

## Failure modes and costs

- Some later claims and popular legends inflate what Tesla demonstrated.
- Brilliant invention did not substitute for capital discipline, customer need, or scalable infrastructure.

## Context you cannot copy

Westinghouse engineering, transformers, generators, utilities, and broader AC development were necessary complements.
