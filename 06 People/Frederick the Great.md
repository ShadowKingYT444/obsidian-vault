# Frederick the Great

> A case in extracting disproportionate military power from a small state through disciplined administration, fiscal focus, and risk—while showing how close strategic audacity can come to catastrophe.

## Operating model

- **Objective:** Preserve and enlarge Prussia among stronger neighboring powers.
- **Constraints:** Small population, exposed geography, powerful Austria/Russia/France, and limited strategic depth.

- Maintained a highly drilled army and state system oriented toward rapid mobilization.
- Used interior lines and operational tempo to fight separated enemies.
- Combined personal command with detailed administrative attention.

## Decisions to study

1. Seizing Silesia transformed Prussia's status but created long-term Austrian hostility.
2. The opening of the Seven Years' War was a preventive gamble under tightening coalition pressure.
3. Persistence after severe defeats mattered because enemy coalition politics also changed.

## Failure modes and costs

- Prussia came close to strategic collapse in the Seven Years' War and benefited from the accession of Peter III in Russia.
- Military efficiency rested on a coercive social and fiscal system not separable from its costs.

## Context you cannot copy

Earlier Hohenzollern rulers had already built the fiscal-military state Frederick used.
