# History and biography study contract

Use history as a laboratory for causal reasoning, not as a collection of hero stories.

## Four-object model

Every substantial history study block should connect four objects:

1. **Person** — incentives, capabilities, constraints, decisions, blind spots.
2. **Event** — causal stack, sequence, turning points, outcomes.
3. **System** — institutions, technology, geography, demography, finance, logistics, culture.
4. **Pattern** — a reusable mechanism that survives a change of names and era.

Do not explain an outcome with personality alone when structural causes do substantial work.

## Biography rule

For every admired trait, ask what evidence shows causation, what the trait cost, what context made it work, where it failed, whether survivorship bias is present, and what a capable rival could have done with the same resources.

## Event rule

Separate structural pressures, enabling conditions, proximate triggers, individual decisions, and contingency.

## Transfer rule

Use `historical case -> mechanism -> boundary conditions -> modern analogy`. Transfer the mechanism, not the costume.

## Source rule

Prefer primary sources plus modern scholarly synthesis. Popular biographies are useful for vivid detail but must not be the sole support for large causal claims.

## Retrieval rule

Predict decisions, compare leaders under similar constraints, remove the famous person from the story, and explain failures with the same framework used for successes.
